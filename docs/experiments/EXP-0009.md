## Observations

- **On the headline metric, on the split with multi-document questions, there is no
  measurable difference**: 15 questions gained, 16 lost, Δ −0.005, p = 1.0. Under either
  reading of P2-04 the candidate is not promoted — `dev_large` says +0.0085 and `dev`
  says −0.005, so the two splits disagree in direction, and `dev` alone is null.
- **The `dev_large` gain is a `feature_request` effect.** Every one of the 67 net flips
  on that split is a "Request:"-type article; on ordinary articles the candidate is
  slightly behind (−0.003, n.s.). Synthetic questions on feature-request articles are
  where text-embedding-3-large separates from qwen — a property of how those articles
  (short, templated) embed, not evidence about support questions.
- **Ranking within the top 5 is better even where set membership is not.** On `dev`,
  strict recall@1 +0.060 (p = 0.078) and nDCG@10 +0.035 (p = 0.071), and nDCG on
  single-document questions +0.045 (p = 0.043). The gold document tends to sit higher,
  which the generator's context (five documents) does not see and Tier 2 would not
  reward. On multi-document questions nothing moves (nDCG −0.005; strict@1 is 0.000 for
  both, by construction — one document at k=1 cannot cover two gold).
- **Flipped `dev` questions are rank shuffles near the boundary**, not retrieval
  failures: gained *"customizing the layout of my products in a grid gallery"* (gold
  rank 9 → 1), *"start a new website design from scratch"* (7 → 3), the two-gold
  *"business email not being directed to my Gmail"* (12, 5 → 5, 2); lost *"pre-set Wix
  website designs"* (5 → 12), *"get my invoices for services paid"* (2 → 16), *"make my
  booking hours live"* (2 → 8). The same gold article (`da2dea…`) is gained on one
  phrasing and lost on another.
- The index is smaller (101 MB vs 134.6 MB; 3072 vs 4096 dims), built five times faster
  (245 s vs 1,213 s, no retries), and queries have a flat latency tail. It costs 13x
  more per token ($0.13 vs $0.01/Mtok): **$0.40 per index vs $0.03**.
- Collapse ratio p90 rose to 1.40 (control 1.20) on both splits: this model ranks
  adjacent chunks of one article closer together more often, so the walk to five
  distinct documents scans more chunks. Exhaustion stayed at 0.

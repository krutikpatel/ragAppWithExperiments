## Question

P1-03: when `top_k` means five *distinct documents* rather than five chunks, how many
chunks does the walk have to scan to find them on this corpus (the collapse ratio), and does
a 50-chunk candidate pool ever run out? This is a measurement of retrieval granularity, not
of retrieval quality.

## Observations

**On this corpus, under BM25, top-5 chunks almost are top-5 documents.** 138 of 200
questions needed no walk at all; the mean ratio is 1.088 and the 90th percentile scans six
chunks for five documents. The Phase 1 handover's premise that "a naive top-5-chunks
retriever structurally caps strict recall on multi-hop questions" is, for this retriever,
smaller than expected: with 79.0% of articles fitting in one 600-word chunk (corpus profile),
most ranked chunks are whole articles and cannot collapse. The multi-document slice has the
*lowest* mean ratio (1.075) and the highest p90 (1.40) — its questions mostly hit distinct
short articles, with a heavier tail.

**Where collapse does happen, it is long payment and bookings articles.** The 2.8 question
("trouble verifying my business Wix Account to receive payments") had its ranking dominated
by *Completing Your Wix Payments Account Setup* (4,333 words, eight chunks) and *Wix Payments
Verification Process* (1,423 words, the gold document). The walk found five distinct
documents at chunk 14; the top-5-chunk context would have held two. The 1.6 question ("remove
the ribbon from one plan on the plan card list") had both gold documents — *Customizing the
Look of Your Pricing Plans* (1,179 words) and *Managing Product Ribbons* (1,026) — in five
distinct documents after eight chunks. Both are cases where the document-level walk changes
what the generator sees; a Tier 2 run of this config is what would show whether that matters,
and none has been run.

**`gold_in_context` equals strict recall@5 on every question.** Since the walk selects the
same first five documents that `max` pooling ranks, the two are the same measurement seen
from the ranking side and the context side. They will diverge only when the pool exhausts
(never here) or the context assembler truncates (not measured in Tier 1).

**The candidate pool of 50 was never approached.** The deepest walk scanned 14 chunks.
OQ-021 asked whether 50 is enough; for BM25 on `dev` it is more than three times the
observed maximum. Whether dense retrieval collapses more — a model that clusters an
article's chunks together would — is not known until the dense control runs.

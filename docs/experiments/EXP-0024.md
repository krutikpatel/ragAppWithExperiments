## Question

Does re-scoring the top 50 candidate documents with a cross-encoder improve
retrieval over the dense control on this corpus, and what does it cost?

A cross-encoder reads the question and the passage *together* in one pass, where the
dense retriever turns each into a vector separately and compares them. It is far
more accurate per pair and far too slow to run over a corpus, so it only ever
re-orders a short candidate list. That also fixes its ceiling: it cannot retrieve
anything the dense retriever did not already return.

## Configuration diff

One dimension against `configs/promoted.yaml` (recorded on the run row as
`vs_promoted_json`, `dimensions: ["reranking"]`):

```yaml
+ reranker: openrouter
+ reranker_params:
+   model: cohere/rerank-v3.5
+   provider: Cohere
+ rerank_candidates: 50     # DISTINCT DOCUMENTS (P1-03), not chunks
```

`rerank_candidates` counts documents, so the reranker saw every ranked chunk of the
top 50 articles — a measured **58.4 chunks per query** (min 50, max 84). The
reranked list was then re-pooled and re-walked for `top_k` = 5 distinct documents,
so every number below, including the collapse ratio, is post-rerank.

## Observations

**The shape is a trade, not a failure.** The reranker is better at the very top of
the list and worse in the middle: +0.065 at rank 1, −0.055 at rank 5, and **exactly
zero at rank 20**. nDCG@10 and MRR both move up while recall@5 moves down, which is
the same fact seen from two angles — the gold document is more often first and less
often somewhere in the top five.

**Rank 20 being identical is the ceiling, measured.** 0.920 → 0.920, 9 questions
gained and 9 lost, p = 1.000. The reranker only re-orders 50 candidate documents, so
the recall of that candidate set is the most it can ever deliver. This is worth
stating plainly because it bounds the whole axis: no reranker, however good, beats
0.920 here without a wider candidate set or a better retriever.

**It moves a great deal in both directions.** Tracking the gold's rank on the 160
single-gold questions:

| before \ after | 1 | 2–5 | 6–20 | 21+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 43 | 14 | 4 | 0 |
| **2–5** | 24 | 26 | 15 | 4 |
| **6–20** | 6 | 5 | 8 | 2 |
| **21+** | 1 | 2 | 2 | 3 |

**40 golds moved up a band, 39 moved down.** The gold's rank was unchanged for only
55 of 160 questions, and 17 questions moved it by 10 or more places (8 up, 9 down).
The top-5 context changed for 194 of 200 questions — a mean of 2.28 of the 5
documents replaced. So the flat aggregate is not the reranker declining to act; it
is a lot of action that nets out.

Reading the queries that flipped (28 lost, 17 gained):

- **Lost.** *"how to add more static pages to a Wix website"* — gold rank 1 → 8.
  *"How to make the published changes draft?"* — 5 → 20. *"How can I offer free
  shipping for a sample option of a product…"* — 5 → 21. *"Is domain privacy
  included in the price…"* — 1 → 7. These are well-formed questions the dense
  retriever had already answered, and the cross-encoder demoted the right article
  confidently, by 6 to 16 places. Three of the six losses examined started at rank 1.
- **Gained.** *"Hoe can I restore the selection of projects for my Wix collection?"*
  — golds at 6 and 20 → 1 and 2. *"do I have to give my login to a website designer
  to work on my site"* — 33 → 2. *"i cant create a new gallery in my website. it only
  refers to portfolio templates"* — 24 → 1. *"I am trying to cancel my Premium
  subscription within the 14-day period, but I cant find the cancel feature."*
  — 6 → 1. The rescues are questions phrased loosely, with typos, or as a complaint
  rather than a query — cases where the embedding comparison did badly and reading
  the question against the passage did well.

That pattern (cross-encoder rescues the badly-phrased, disturbs the already-correct)
is a description of 45 questions, not a measured law. It is written up as OQ-037
with the test that would settle it, not as a conclusion.

**The collapse ratio barely moved**: 1.106 → 1.113, p90 unchanged at 1.40, zero pool
exhaustion. The concern P2-10 raises — a cross-encoder re-concentrating the context
onto one article and undoing the retriever's document diversity — **did not
materialise here**. The corpus explains it: 79% of articles are a single chunk, so
there usually is no second chunk of the same article to promote.

**Cost and latency are the clearest result.** 4x the median latency and ~2,900x the
per-query cost, for no measurable change in retrieval quality. On `dev` that is
$0.23; the same configuration on `dev_large` would be $7.28.

## Anomalies

- **A VOID run preceded this one.** `run_20260923_052903_c2ea` died on its first
  query embedding: `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` was not exported in the launching shell. No
  provider call was made and nothing was spent. The runner recorded it VOID rather
  than dropping it; it stays in the ledger. See MIS-026.
- **This run is recorded `git_dirty=1`, and the dirt was not code.** The tree was
  clean at launch; `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md` was then edited — to record the VOID row
  above — inside the window before the runner read git state. `git diff f337ae2`
  over that window touches one file, `docs/EXPERIMENTS.md`, and no file under
  `rag/`, `configs/` or `prompts/`. So `f337ae2` does describe the code that
  produced these numbers, but the flag on the row is correct and stays. See MIS-027.
- **The cost estimate was 17% low**: $0.2001 estimated, $0.2341 billed — 234 Cohere
  search units for 200 queries, not the 200 assumed. Under the $2 gate either way,
  and the estimator has been recalibrated against this run (MIS-028, OQ-036).
- **Two transient retries** on the rerank endpoint, zero rate-limited (HTTP 429).
  Every call was served by the pinned provider, Cohere.
- No question failed. 200 of 200 scored.

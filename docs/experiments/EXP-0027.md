## Question

Maximal Marginal Relevance re-orders candidates by
`λ·relevance − (1−λ)·max similarity to an already-picked candidate`. P2-13
prioritises it because diversity should serve multi-document coverage — the slice
that is 20% of `dev` and 46% of its failures. Does trading relevance for diversity
put more distinct gold documents in the context?

## Observations

**The identity check validates the machinery.** λ=1.0 reduces MMR to the retriever's
own ranking, and it reproduces the control: 0.715 / collapse 1.107 / nDCG 0.6304
against the control's 0.715–0.720 / 1.106 / 0.6305, inside the dense retriever's
known non-determinism (spread 0.005, OQ-023). So the curve below it is the technique,
not a bug. `strict recall@1` is also flat at ~0.30 across every λ, exactly as it must
be — MMR's first pick has nothing to be redundant with, so it is always the most
relevant candidate.

**MMR did what it was asked and it was the wrong thing.** The collapse ratio falls
1.106 → **1.000** at λ ≤ 0.5: the context became five chunks from five distinct
documents, every time, with no exhaustion. Perfect document diversity. Recall halved.
Diversity was never the binding constraint — the control's collapse ratio was already
1.106, which is to say the context was already 90% distinct documents, so there was
almost nothing for MMR to fix and a great deal for it to break.

**Why it breaks: the redundancy term has more dynamic range than the relevance term.**
Measured over 60 questions' 50-document candidate sets:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| relevance (query–chunk cosine) spread *within* a candidate set | **0.173** |
| pairwise chunk–chunk cosine, all pairs | mean **0.667** (p5 0.505, p95 0.832) |
| pairwise cosine, two chunks of the *same* document | 0.873 |
| pairwise cosine, chunks of *different* documents | 0.666 |

Every candidate is about two-thirds similar to every other candidate, and the
redundancy term ranges over roughly 0.5–0.87 while relevance ranges over 0.173. So at
λ=0.5 the diversity term has more than twice the leverage of the relevance term, and
the ordering stops being "most relevant, minus duplicates" and becomes "least like
anything already picked" — which on a single-domain help centre selects **semantic
outliers**. That is the whole failure in one sentence: on a corpus where everything is
about one product, being unlike the other candidates is evidence of irrelevance.

**And the multi-document collapse has its own measurement.** For multi-gold questions
with two or more golds in the candidate set:

- cosine between **two gold documents of the same question**: **0.788**
- cosine between a gold and the average non-gold candidate: **0.693**

A second gold document is **+0.095 more similar to the first gold** than an average
candidate is — naturally, because both articles are about the same task. MMR's
redundancy term penalises similarity to what is already picked, so **it penalises the
second gold harder than it penalises an irrelevant document.** The technique
prioritised for multi-document coverage is structurally biased against multi-document
answers on this corpus. multi-doc 0.350 → 0.000 at λ=0.3 is that bias run to
completion: 14 questions lost, 0 gained, not one multi-gold question survived.

**What would have to be true for MMR to help here.** Gold documents for one question
would need to be *less* similar to each other than to the surrounding candidates —
the opposite of what was measured. That is plausible for a corpus spanning several
domains; it is not true of a single product's help centre.

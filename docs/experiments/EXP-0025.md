## Question

Same question as EXP-0024, different reranker: does `cohere/rerank-4-fast`
(`rerank-v4.0-fast`, 32k context against v3.5's 4k) improve retrieval over the dense
control? And — the question EXP-0024 could not answer on its own — do two
cross-encoders differ enough on this corpus that a single one's null result says
anything about reranking in general (OQ-035)?

## Configuration diff

One dimension against `configs/promoted.yaml`, identical to EXP-0024 except the model:

```yaml
+ reranker: openrouter
+ reranker_params:
+   model: cohere/rerank-4-fast
+   provider: Cohere
+ rerank_candidates: 50
```

Same 50 candidate documents, same 58.4 candidate chunks per query, same re-pooling.

## Observations

**Two cross-encoders, same candidate set, significantly different results.** 24
questions had the gold in the top 5 under `rerank-4-fast` but not under `v3.5`, and 11
the other way. This retrospectively justifies the caveat attached to EXP-0024 and
DEC-059: a single reranker's null result was never evidence about reranking. It is now
measured that it was not.

**It is less destructive than v3.5, not more accurate.** Gold rank-band migration on
the 160 single-gold questions:

| before \ after | 1 | 2–5 | 6–20 | 21+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 45 | 12 | 4 | 0 |
| **2–5** | 21 | 32 | 15 | 1 |
| **6–20** | 8 | 6 | 7 | 0 |
| **21+** | 1 | 2 | 1 | 4 |

39 up, 32 down — against v3.5's 40 up, 39 down. The gains are almost identical; what
differs is that `4-fast` throws away less. It still demoted 16 golds out of rank 1,
and still replaced 2.27 of the 5 context documents per question, with only 3 of 200
contexts left untouched.

**The ceiling is not where EXP-0024 said it was.** This run reached strict recall@20 =
**0.940**, above the control's 0.920, which EXP-0024 called the reranker's ceiling. It
is not: the reranker re-orders the top **50** documents, so a gold sitting at dense
rank 21–50 can be promoted into the top 20, and eight were. The real ceiling is the
candidate set's own recall, measured on the control: **strict recall@50 = 0.985**.
See MIS-029; EXP-0024's claim is corrected there and in place.

That number reframes the whole axis. The gold document is in the 50-document candidate
set for **98.5%** of questions, and the best reranker here surfaces it in the top five
for **73%**. The gap is not a retrieval problem — the documents are already in hand —
it is a ranking problem that neither cross-encoder solved.

**Latency got worse, not better, despite the name.** `rerank-4-fast` ran at a p50 of
3,847 ms against v3.5's 1,194 ms and the control's 298 ms — 13x the control. Per-call
mean latency was almost identical between the two rerankers (691 ms vs 654 ms), so the
p50 gap is queueing and variance at the provider on the day, not model speed; the p95
of **18,774 ms** — nearly 30x the control's 635 ms, against a 691 ms mean per call —
says the same much louder: a handful of calls stalled for many seconds. Latency measured on one afternoon against one provider is
not a property of the model, and it is not treated as one here.

**Cost:** $0.4481 actual against a $0.4681 estimate — **4% under**, the first
out-of-sample test of the search-unit recalibration from MIS-028, which was calibrated
on EXP-0024 and had never been checked against different data. 224 units for 200
queries against the calibrated 234. One check is not validation, but it is no longer
uncalibrated (OQ-036).

## Anomalies

- **`git_dirty=0`.** Clean at launch and at git read, unlike EXP-0024. The batch's
  *second* run was not (MIS-030).
- The estimator's `candidate_words` uses the corpus-wide mean chunk length (309
  words) where the actual candidates average 348, because longer articles produce
  more chunks and so are retrieved more often. It did not matter for a search-unit
  model; it will matter for a token-billed reranker.
- One transient retry, zero rate-limited. Every call served by the pinned provider.
- No question failed. 200 of 200 scored.

## Question

How often does the naive system, which has no refusal mechanism, produce an answer to a
question that nothing in the corpus can answer?

## Observations

Written after reading all 45 answers.

**The baseline refuses far more than expected — for two of the three reasons.** Every
other-platform question was refused (15 of 15 by hand; 4 of those in wording the detector
missed). Post-snapshot questions were refused 13 of 15. The prompt's fixed phrase and the
"use ONLY the articles" instruction were enough for questions whose answer is visibly absent
from the retrieved text. This does not say the refusals are *good*: several add "I would need
a Wix Help Center article about Webflow" or offer to summarise Wix's process instead, which
a user would experience as unhelpful rather than as honest.

**Underspecified questions are where the false answers live: 10 of 15 answered, all with
citations.** *"How do I get it approved?"* → a numbered procedure for approving Online
Programs members, cited. *"Why was my account charged?"* → a confident explanation about
subscription renewals, cited. *"Where do I click?"* → five citations and a list of steps for
some page in the Editor. *"Can I undo it?"* → "Yes. You can undo actions in the Wix Editor…"
The retriever returns five plausible articles for any short question, and the generator
treats their presence as evidence the question is about them. This is the failure Phase 2
refusal logic is measured against; the number is **10 of 15**, or 12 of 45 overall.

**Two post-snapshot false answers assert dates the articles do not contain.** *"Which Wix
Bookings features shipped after December 2024?"* → a specific feature "shipped after
December 2024", cited to an article that has no date. *"What shipping-rule changes did Wix
Stores announce in 2025?"* → describes a UI change as a 2025 announcement. The corpus snapshot
is 2024-12-02; nothing in it can date a change to 2025. These two are the clearest ungrounded
claims in the run.

**The generator copied the prompt's own instruction into two answers.** *"How do I restore a
deleted page in Squarespace?"* → "The provided articles do not cover this. Then, in one
sentence, say what is missing: They do not include instructions…". The phrase "Then, in one
sentence, say what is missing" is the prompt's text, not an answer. Twice in 45. A `v2`
prompt should phrase that rule as a description, not an imperative the model can echo. Frozen
for the phase (DEC-042).

**Citations on a refusal.** Two lexical refusals cited articles anyway (*"How much does it
cost?"* cited five while saying pricing "is not covered"). The prompt says "do not cite
anything" on a refusal; the model does not always comply. Harmless for citation precision
here (recall is N/A, precision counts them as wrong, as it should).

## Anomalies

- **One citation with a space inside the hex id** — `[doc:4e a4e961…]` in the answer to
  *"Why isn't my site working?"*. Swept across EXP-0006, EXP-0007 and EXP-0001's canonical
  Tier 2 run: **one instance in 245 answers**. Not parsed, not counted; a third citation
  shape after MIS-016. Left as is — one instance does not justify a parser version.
- The recorded `cost_per_query_usd` (0.0091) is the pre-run estimate with a judge that was
  then skipped; the estimator now takes `n_judged` and would report $0.0153 total for this
  run. The row is not rewritten.
- No judge failures (none called), no generation retries, no URLs.

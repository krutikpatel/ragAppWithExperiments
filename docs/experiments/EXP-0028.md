## Question
Does writing the five selected documents into the prompt strongest-at-both-ends
instead of in rank order change the answers the generator produces?

## Why no retrieval numbers appear below
This technique reorders the *assembled context*. It cannot change which documents were
retrieved, ranked or selected, so P2-13 forbids reporting retrieval deltas for it and
none are reported. That is a property of the code, not a promise:
`reorder_lost_in_middle` returns a permutation of its input, and
`tests/test_assembly_p2_13.py` asserts that for every length from 0 to 11.

Measured confirmation that nothing upstream moved: the top-5 **document set was
identical on 98 of 100 questions**. The two that differ are hosted-embedding
non-determinism (OQ-023), the same effect that gives the control's own three runs a
0.010 range on strict recall@5 — not this change.

## Observations

**The prompt is shorter than the shortest context the technique was measured on.**
The assembled context averages **1,712 words ≈ 2,174 tokens** across the 100 questions
(`context_words`, recorded per question by this run). Liu et al. 2023 measured the
mid-prompt accuracy dip across contexts of roughly **2,700 to 21,000 tokens** with 10
to 30 retrieved documents. This configuration assembles **five** documents into ~2,174
tokens. There may simply be no middle here to get lost in, and that is a fact about
this corpus's configuration rather than about the technique.

**It changed 13 individual answers and zero metrics.** 15 of 100 questions changed
their citation recall. For **13 of those 15 the retrieved document set and its ranking
were byte-identical** between the two runs — the only difference was the position of
those same five documents in the prompt. The other two had a different retrieved set
and are OQ-023 embedding noise, not this change. Six of the thirteen went up, seven
went down. The technique therefore produces real churn at the level of individual
answers and cancels itself out completely in aggregate.

**Reordering the same five documents flipped one answer to the opposite claim.**
Question `3f42c8a9ba580aaf` ("migrating a Wix Editor site to Wix Studio"). Identical
five documents, identical ranking. The control answered *"you cannot migrate an
existing Wix Editor site to Wix Studio; you'll need to rebuild your site in the Studio
Editor"*, citing the gold document. This run answered *"In the editor, follow the prompt
banner or instructions to update to Wix Studio"*, citing a different document, and
described a migration path the gold article says does not exist. Nothing about the
evidence changed — only which document led the prompt. This is not a metric-level
finding (step coverage p=0.79), but it is the clearest single illustration in this
project so far that the generator's answer is a function of context *order* and not
only of context *content*.

**Two questions flipped into refusal and one out of it.** `156e17954eb1d680` and
`a9d7e4aae41b0b15` both went from a correctly-cited answer to *"The provided articles
do not cover this"* with the same documents present; `a7fecaf9f7855d20` went the other
way, from refusal to a correct three-document answer. Net refusal change −0.01,
p=1.000. The refusal detector is stable here (`refusal-lexical-v2`); these are genuine
changes in the model's behaviour, not detection artifacts.

**Latency is not comparable and should not be read as an improvement.** p95 was
5,441 ms against the control's 17,448 ms, but the control's three runs range over
32,030 ms and those runs called the judge. Assembly ordering does no extra work; this
number reflects provider conditions, not the technique.

## Anomalies
The `q_len:long` slice moved further than any other on both citation precision
(+0.0660, p=0.403) and step coverage (+0.0657, p=0.497) — the only slice where both
moved up together, and the only place a position effect would be expected to appear
first if one existed, since long questions retrieve longer contexts. Both are well
inside noise at n=26 and n=11 and neither is a finding. Noted because it is the one
direction worth re-checking if this technique is ever revisited at a larger `top_k`.

## Hypothesis
H-020, written before the run: *reordering changes no deterministic generation metric
by more than its noise floor*, on the reasoning that the prompt is too short for the
effect. **Confirmed**, and the reasoning was measurable rather than lucky: the context
came in at 2,174 tokens against the paper's 2,700-token floor. The part I flagged as
most likely wrong — that citation *recall* is where a position effect would show — was
also right to flag: recall is exactly where the 13-question churn appeared, at a net
of −0.0050 and p=1.000.

## Observations

**The gap is split difficulty, not overfitting, and the evidence is the direction of the
asymmetry.** Overfitting has a signature: the configuration that was *selected* on dev
degrades more than one that was not, because it carries the noise that made it look good.
The opposite happened. **BM25 — which has no fitted parameters at all beyond `k1` and `b`
at their library defaults, and which was never tuned on anything — lost 0.0650, while the
dense control lost 0.0400.** An unselected baseline cannot be overfitted, so its larger
drop can only be a property of the split. That the selected arm moved *less* than the
unselected one is the cleanest evidence available that little selection pressure was ever
applied.

**And the ledger says why.** 58 runs were executed against the same 200 dev questions,
which is the shape of programme that normally produces a large optimism gap. It did not,
because the usual mechanism is selection and **selection never operated**: every axis
returned "no measurable difference" or worse, the promote-on-significant-positive rule
fired zero times, and `promoted.yaml` has the same identity hash today as the day it was
written. A configuration never chosen for its dev score cannot be overfitted to it.

**Three numbers came back exactly identical**, which is worth stating because it is the
kind of thing that looks like a bug: `strict recall@20` (0.9200 both), Tier 2
`gold_in_context` (0.6700 both) and Tier 2 refusal rate (0.0800 both). They are computed
from different questions and different answers. Checked: the runs have different
`run_id`s, different `split_hash`es and different per-question rows.

**The largest single gap is a generation metric, and it moved equally in both arms.**
Citation precision fell 0.1164 for dense and 0.1229 for sparse. A change that lands on
both retrievers equally is not about retrieval — it is a property of the test questions
and their articles. It is also the metric whose noise floor is the widest this project
measured (MDD 0.080 on the dense control, where the generator rather than the judge is
the noisy instrument), so a 0.12 move is closer to 1.5 noise floors than to a finding.

**Where the system is genuinely worse on test:** the single-document slice, −0.0609. The
multi-document slice went *up* 0.0346 — on 39 and 40 questions respectively, so neither
is distinguishable from noise, but the direction is worth recording: the slice this
project spent six axes failing to improve is the slice that transferred best.

## Hypothesis
H-034, written before the split was opened. **Right on the dense arm and on the headline
finding; wrong on the sparse arm, in exactly the way I named as the falsifier.**
- Predicted dense within ±0.05 of 0.720 → **0.6800, gap −0.0400.** Correct.
- Predicted the dense-over-sparse gap survives above +0.20 → **+0.3350.** Correct.
- Predicted sparse within ±0.05 of 0.410 → **0.3450, gap −0.0650.** **Wrong**, outside
  the band.
- I wrote: *"the part I expect to be wrong is the sparse control. BM25 is a lexical
  method with no fitted parameters… if anything moves more than the dense arm, my account
  of where the exposure lies is wrong."* BM25 moved more. **My account of where the
  exposure lies was wrong** — I attributed the gap to the four or five Phase 1 selection
  decisions, and the arm with *zero* selection decisions moved furthest. The gap is
  the two splits differing, not the config fitting.
  Being wrong here strengthens the conclusion rather than weakening it, which is an
  uncomfortable thing to write and the honest reading.

## Decision
**`promoted.yaml` unchanged. Phase 2 closed. Nothing tuned in response to these numbers,
and the split is not burned.** The project's headline number is now **strict recall@5 =
0.68 on 200 unseen questions.**

the three that is token-billed rather than per search unit. Does an open-weight
cross-encoder behave differently on this corpus?

## Observations

**The cleanest null in the project.** 26 gained, 26 lost, delta exactly 0.0000,
p = 1.000. The reranker changed the answer for a quarter of the question set and
moved the metric by nothing at all. It is a good illustration of why this project
counts flips as well as deltas: a delta of zero here describes 52 changed outcomes,
not an inert component.

**It is the only one of the three that is worse at rank 1.** −0.020 (p = 0.687),
where both Cohere models gained ~0.07 there. Its shape runs the other way: flat at 5,
**+0.030 at 10 and +0.030 at 20** — the best deep recall in the axis, and the highest
recall@20 any configuration in this project has produced (0.950 against the control's
0.920). So the three rerankers do not share a failure mode. Two sharpen the very top
at the cost of the middle; this one improves the middle and slightly blunts the top.

**Across the axis, no reranker moved the deciding metric and all three changed a
great deal.** The per-model verdicts are −0.055 (p = 0.139), +0.010 (p = 0.887) and
+0.000 (p = 1.000). Whatever a cross-encoder is doing to this candidate set, it is
not the thing strict recall@5 measures.

**The ceiling stands, and none of them approached it.** The candidate set's recall is
**0.985** (control, @50). The three rerankers delivered 0.665, 0.730 and 0.720 at
k=5. The gap identified in MIS-029 is untouched by any of them — F16.

**Cost was 32% over estimate**, the largest miss yet, and for a reason specific to
this billing model: a cross-encoder scores (query, document) **pairs**, so the query
and the model's prompt template are billed once *per document*. Measured at **517.9
tokens per pair** against candidates averaging 348 words (442 tokens) — roughly 76
tokens of per-pair overhead, multiplied by 58 candidates, is a third of the bill. The
estimator has been corrected and now reproduces the run to within 0.1% (MIS-032,
OQ-036). At $0.00605 per query this is by far the most expensive component this
project has run — 15,000x the control's per-query cost, and 2.7x the more expensive
Cohere model.

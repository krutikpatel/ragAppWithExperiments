## Question
Does retrieving for a more general form of the question alongside the original, fused by
RRF, surface overview articles a specific phrasing walks past (Zheng et al. 2023)?

## Configuration diff
```yaml
+ query_transform: step_back
+ query_transform_params: {model: openai/gpt-oss-20b}
```
One general question per question, retrieved for alongside the original. 200/200
generated, zero fallbacks.

## Observations

**The predicted defect is real and far more common than the probe suggested — and it
explains only a quarter of the damage.** H-026 was written from a 6-question probe in
which 2 step-back questions named "Wix Bookings" for questions that named no product.
Measured across all 200:

| | count |
|---|---|
| Invented a Wix product the question never named | **46 (23.0%)** |
| Kept a product the question did name | 40 |
| Named no Wix product at all | 114 |

Examples: *"How to make the published changes draft?"* → *"How does **Wix Bookings**
handle published changes draft?"*; *"how to reorder plan options during checkout in
Wix"* → *"How does **Wix Bookings** handle reordering plan options during checkout?"*.
The prompt instructs the model to keep the product name; where there is none, it
supplies one, and "Bookings" is its default guess.

**But only 6 of those 46 questions lost the gold (13%), against 25 losses in total.**
So the hallucinated product name accounts for **roughly a quarter** of the regression.
The hypothesis named a real defect and over-attributed the damage to it. That correction
matters more than the prediction being directionally right.

**The rest is the same fusion problem the whole axis has.** The step-back question
retrieved **34.7 documents per question that the original did not** — it is doing
exactly what it is supposed to do, finding the general/overview material. Fused by RRF
at equal weight, "best overview article" then outranks "good specific article", and the
gold on this corpus is almost always the specific one. A user asking why their payout is
on hold wants the payout-hold article, not the payments overview.

**Long questions suffer most** (−0.129, p = 0.037), the same slice decomposition hurt
worst, and for the same reason: a long specific question has the most specificity to
lose when it is generalised.

## Hypothesis
H-026: **direction correct, magnitude under-stated, mechanism over-attributed.** I
predicted a fall of more than 0.010 (the re-embedding floor); it fell **0.075 at
p = 0.0169**. I predicted the cause was product-name invention; that happens at 23%, far
above the rate I would have guessed, but explains only 6 of 25 losses. I also wrote "if
the rate is low the effect may be invisible" — the rate was high and the effect is
significant, but not mostly *through that channel*. Being right for partly the wrong
reason is worth recording as clearly as being wrong.

## Decision
**Control retained; not promoted.** Significant and negative.

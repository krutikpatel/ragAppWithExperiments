## Question
Does a second model call that inspects the answer against its own context, and replaces
an unsupported answer with a refusal, reduce false answers — and what does it cost?

## Configuration diff
```yaml
+ grounding_check: self_check
+ grounding_check_params: {model: openai/gpt-oss-20b, max_tokens: 200}
```
The generator prompt is the **control's**, so this measures the check rather than a
different way of asking.

## Observations

**It trades two false refusals for every false answer it prevents.** False answers fell
by 0.0444 — two questions of 45. False refusals rose by 0.0913 — six questions of 66.
On the raw counts the check rejected 12 dev answers, of which **6 were answers to
questions whose gold document was right there in the context**. It is rejecting roughly
as much good work as bad.

**It is the wrong instrument aimed at the right problem, and the curve says so.** The
abstention threshold (EXP-0038) reaches a false-answer rate of **0.1333 at a
false-refusal rate of 0.0299** — better on *both* axes than this check's 0.2222 / 0.1212
— for **no LLM call and no added latency**. A technique that is dominated on both axes
of its own trade-off by a free alternative does not need a further argument against it.

**It never failed, and it never got confused.** Zero unparseable verdicts, zero failed
calls, and every one of the 41 refusals across both splits was short-circuited without a
call, exactly as designed. The mechanics are sound; the judgment is not.

**Citation recall fell 0.0917 and `cited_nothing` rose 0.11** — both are downstream of
the same nine rejections, since a rejected answer becomes a refusal that cites nothing.
They are not independent findings.

## Anomalies
On the pre-run probe, one `unanswerable` question the generator had **answered** was
judged SUPPORTED by the check — the exact failure it exists to catch, missed on a
10-answer sample. The full run bears that out: the check caught 2 of the 12 false
answers the control produced on `unanswerable`, and rejected 6 good dev answers doing it.

## Hypothesis
H-029 predicted the false-answer rate would fall by **less than 0.10** (it fell 0.0444 —
**correct**) and the dev rejection rate would be **under 0.20** (it was 0.1200 —
**correct**). The reasoning was also right: I wrote that "a model asked whether its own
family's output is grounded in a context that was retrieved to look relevant is being
asked a question it is poorly placed to answer", and flagged the probe observation that
drove it. What I did not predict is the **size of the false-refusal cost** — +0.0913,
three times the control's entire false-refusal rate. I priced the technique's latency
and money in advance and not its quality cost.

## Decision
**Control retained; not promoted.** Dominated on both axes by a free alternative.

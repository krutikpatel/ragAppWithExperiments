## Question

What does the dumbest defensible retriever score on this corpus, so that every later
technique has a number to beat?

## Configuration

```yaml
retriever: bm25            # rank-bm25 Okapi, k1=1.5, b=0.75
                           # tokenizer: lowercase [a-z0-9]+, no stemming, no stopwords
chunker: fixed_token       # 512 whitespace words, overlap 0
retrieval_depth: 100       # ranked for scoring, so k up to 20 is real (DEC-012)
top_k: 5                   # what a generator would see
doc_pooling: max
split: dev
eval_tier: tier1
seed: 1
```

## Observations

Factual statements about what the numbers show. What was varied here is nothing —
this is the baseline — so there are no causal claims to make, only descriptions of
the failure shape that later experiments will be judged on changing.

**The failure is mostly total, not partial.** At k=5, 81 questions have every gold
document, 20 have some, and **99 have none**. Half the questions get nothing useful
in the top five.

**Multi-document questions fail on the second document.** On the 40 multi-gold
questions, loose recall@5 is 0.675 but strict is 0.175. BM25 finds *one* of the
required documents for two thirds of them and *all* of them for fewer than a fifth.
This is the gap strict recall exists to expose (DEC-010), and it is the largest
single-slice effect in the run.

**Most misses are ranking failures, not vocabulary failures — but a third are the
latter.** Of the 136 gold documents missing from a top-5, 87 are present somewhere in
the top-100 (median rank 18: 26 at ranks 6-10, 25 at 11-20, 36 at 21-100) and 49 are
not in the top-100 at all. So roughly two thirds of misses are documents BM25 scored
but ranked below five competitors, and one third are documents BM25 did not score
highly enough to surface in a hundred.

**Question length is not monotonic.** Short questions (≤12 words, n=74) score 0.473,
medium (13-17, n=64) score 0.328, long (≥18, n=62) score 0.403. The medium tercile is
worst; the reason is not established by this run.

**Expert-written and simulated questions score the same on strict recall (0.410 vs
0.400) but differ on loose (0.560 vs 0.450).** The two sources fail differently at the
partial-hit level even though their headline number is the same.

**What the failing questions look like.** Six of the 119 questions with a gold
document outside the top-5, read in full:

1. *"prevent an item in my CMS collection from being published … before it's
   completed"* — gold ("Controlling Live Site Item Visibility") at rank 14. The
   question says *published*; the article says *visibility*.
2. *"how to add more static pages to a Wix website"* — gold ("Adding a Page to Your
   Site") **not in the top-100**. Top-1 was "Converting a Dynamic Page to a Regular
   Static Page": the query's rarest term, *static*, matched an article about
   something else.
3. *"restore the selection of projects for my Wix collection"* — two gold docs, at
   ranks 4 and 7. Strict miss, loose hit. Top-1 was a Portfolio article matched on
   *projects*.
4. *"payment settings … payouts are on hold"* — two gold docs at ranks 2 and 9; top-1
   was "About Payouts on Hold", which is topically right and is not a gold document.
5. *"How to make the published changes draft?"* — gold ("Saving, Previewing and
   Publishing Your Site") at rank 59. Top-1 was a Blog article: *draft* and
   *published* are blog vocabulary in this corpus.
6. *"free shipping for a sample option of a product"* — gold at rank 9; top-1 was a
   Fit by Wix merchandise article.

Cases 1, 2 and 5 share a shape: the question's words and the gold article's words
name the same thing differently. Cases 3, 4 and 6 share another: the gold article is
in the top-10 but a topically adjacent article outranks it. These are descriptions of
this run. Whether any technique changes them is what later experiments measure.

### Observations

**The generator cites what it is given, and what it is given is right 40% of the
time.** Citation precision 0.388 against strict recall@5 0.400 on the same questions:
the two numbers are the same thing seen from two ends. 88 of 100 answers carried at
least one well-formed `[doc:<id>]` tag, so the citation-format risk flagged in DEC-017
did not appear on real retrieval either.

**When retrieval fails, the generator answers anyway.** On the 60 questions where
not every gold document reached the top five, the generator refused **twice** and
produced an answer **58 times**. Those 58 answers are grounded in the wrong or
insufficient articles, and every deterministic metric here counts them as answers
rather than as the failure they are.

**The two "false refusals" are correct refusals, and the metric is wrong.** Both
refused questions had `strict_recall@5 = 0`: the articles needed were not retrieved,
and the generator said it could not find the information. That is exactly the
behaviour the answer prompt asks for. `false_refusal` currently means "refused on a
question that has gold documents *in the corpus*"; the honest definition is "refused
on a question whose gold documents *were retrieved*". As defined, the metric penalises
the generator for retrieval's failure. Recorded as MIS-012; the redefinition needs
Krutik's sign-off (CLAUDE.md section 9) and is not applied here.

**Step coverage of 0.123 is not yet a statement about the system.** On the 32
procedural questions, coverage was 0.159 when every gold document was retrieved
(n=14) and 0.096 when not (n=18) — low in both cases. Reading the matched pairs
shows the lexical matcher missing paraphrases: *"Enabling Sandbox Collections"*
against *"Enable Sandbox in your CMS"* scores Jaccard 0.2 on content words, under the
0.5 threshold, because *enabling* and *enable* are different tokens. One question
with all gold documents retrieved scored 1.00 (5 reference steps, 7 generated,
matched in order); another scored 0.00 with 11 reference steps and 3 generated —
genuine under-coverage. The number is a mix of matcher misses and real omissions in
unknown proportion, which is what OQ-007 exists to settle. Until it is settled, this
number describes the matcher as much as the answers.

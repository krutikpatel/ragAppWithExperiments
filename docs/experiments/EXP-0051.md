## Question
The top-k sweep measured the input-side gain exactly: the gold document reaches the
generator for 0.720 of dev questions at `top_k=5` and 0.850 at 10, and that replicated
out of sample (test 0.680 → 0.810). **None of that says what the generator does with the
extra documents.** This run measures only that.

## The answer to OQ-040, and it decomposes exactly

**Fifteen more questions per hundred had their gold document in the prompt, and citation
recall did not rise.** Splitting the hundred questions by whether the gold document was
newly available explains the whole result:

| Group | n | citation recall | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold **newly** in context at k=10 | **15** | 0.1667 → 0.3778 | **+0.2111** |
| Gold **already** in context at k=5 | **67** | 0.8209 → 0.7463 | **−0.0746** |
| Gold in context in **neither** | 18 | 0 → 0 | 0 |
| Gold **lost** at k=10 | **0** | — | — |

`(15 × +0.2111) + (67 × −0.0746) = −0.0183 per 100` — **exactly the observed overall
delta.** The two effects are real, opposite, and the damaging one is 4.5x larger by
population.

**The mechanism works.** On the fifteen questions that gained their gold document,
citation recall more than doubled — five improved, nine were unchanged, one got worse.
Put the right article in front of this generator and it does cite it more.

**And the collateral damage is bigger.** On the sixty-seven questions that already had
their gold document, adding five more articles cost **0.0746 of citation recall and
0.1404 of citation precision**. These were the questions that were already working. The
extra documents are, for them, pure distraction.

## Observations

**This is the clearest measurement of dilution in the project.** Every earlier result
about context length was indirect: EXP-0029 shortened the context and citation recall
fell; EXP-0039 and EXP-0040 made the model describe documents instead of answering. This
one moves the dial in the helpful direction, confirms the input-side gain at +0.150, and
shows the benefit being outweighed by what it does to everything else.

**Citation precision is the metric that breaks, and it breaks significantly** — −0.0851
against an MDD of 0.080, the third significant deterministic result in the project and
the third that is negative. Doubling the documents doubles the opportunities to attribute
a claim to the wrong one, and the generator takes them.

**It explains something the rest of the project could not.** Six axes failed to improve
retrieval, and it was reasonable to read that as "the retrieval ceiling is the problem".
This run improved what reaches the generator by **fifteen points** — far more than any
technique managed — and the answers did not get better. **The bottleneck is not how often
the right document is retrieved. It is what the generator does with a context that
contains it.** That reframes the whole phase: the axes were not failing to find headroom,
they were finding headroom that the generation step cannot use.

**The cost side is unambiguous.** 1.82x the generator input tokens, permanently, per
query. There is no configuration of this trade in which k=10 is free.

## Anomalies
`gold_in_context` rose 0.150 here against the 0.130 the full-split sweep predicted, and
**no question lost its gold document** — the walk is strictly additive in k, as the
document walk's design implies. The subsample is 100 of 200 questions, so a 0.02
difference from the full-split figure is unremarkable.

The runner warned that the `assembly` axis already holds 8 recorded config hashes
against a cap of 5 (the λ sweep contributed five). The warning is correct and is noted
rather than suppressed; this run is the Tier 2 half of an experiment already counted
(DEC-061's reconstructed sweep), not a ninth technique.

## Hypothesis
H-035, written before the run. **Two of three right, and the one I got wrong is the
finding.**
1. Predicted **citation recall rises by more than its MDD of 0.040**, on the reasoning
   that 13 more questions per 100 have their gold document present and a document not in
   the prompt cannot be cited. **Wrong.** It *fell* 0.0183. The premise was correct —
   15 questions gained their gold and their recall rose 0.2111 — and the conclusion did
   not follow, because I only counted the questions that would gain and never asked what
   the same change does to the 67 that were already fine.
2. Predicted **citation precision falls but stays inside its MDD**. Half right: it fell,
   and it went **outside** the MDD at −0.0851. I named the mechanism ("twice as many
   chances to cite a wrong one") and underestimated it.
3. Predicted **step coverage does not move beyond its MDD**. Correct, +0.0104.
- I also wrote that the part I expected to be wrong was "that any of it reaches
  significance". One metric did, and it was the one I had placed inside the noise.

## Decision
**Control retained. `promoted.yaml` unchanged.** `top_k=10` costs 1.82x the generator
tokens per query, permanently, and buys a significant *loss* of citation precision with
no measurable gain in citation recall.

## Question

Does fusing the dense and BM25 rankings by reciprocal rank beat the better of the two
alone, on this corpus, with `top_k` counted in distinct documents (DEC-040)? Fusion
definitions are DEC-056.

## Observations

Read from `rag diff promoted run_20260917_174747_7c9f` with the gold document's rank
under dense, BM25 and RRF for all 52 flipped questions.

- **RRF is a consensus rule, and on this corpus consensus is the wrong bet.** Of the 38
  questions lost, **22 have a gold document that BM25 does not return in its top 100
  at all.** Under RRF a document present in one list only can score at most
  1/(60+1) = 0.0164, while any chunk at ranks (5, 8) in both lists scores 0.030. So a
  document dense ranked **1st** dropped to rank 14–36 whenever BM25 missed it:
  *"how to add more static pages to a Wix website"* (dense 1, BM25 absent, RRF 20);
  *"how can I find my domain information purchased from Wix"* (1, absent, 36); *"What
  should I do if I'm having issues making a payment"* (1, absent, 29). The other 16
  losses have the gold at BM25 rank 16–65 and RRF rank 6–14: still a missing-consensus
  penalty, just a smaller one. Every one of the 1,000 context documents but one came
  from the intersection of the two lists, which is why the counters read 999 / 0 / 1.
- **What it gained is what BM25 was already good at.** 9 of the 14 gained questions were
  BM25-alone hits (BM25 rank 1–5); RRF pulled them into the top five because dense
  also had them, at ranks 6–33: *"add a full PDF to my portfolio site"* (dense 9, BM25 1,
  RRF 1); *"change the header background to black when scrolling"* (8, 1, 1); *"give my
  login to a website designer"* (33, 2, 4). The remaining 5 gains are questions neither
  half answered alone — both had the gold at ranks 6–14 and the sum of two mediocre
  ranks beat the competition (e.g. *"cancel my Premium subscription within the 14-day
  period"*: dense 6, BM25 7, RRF 4). That is the fusion effect the technique is sold
  on; here it is five questions against 38.
- **Multi-document questions did not move** (0.350 → 0.325, 5 / 6, p = 1.0; loose recall
  0.850 both). The loss is entirely on single-gold questions (−0.144), and mostly on
  short questions (−0.176, 14 lost / 1 gained), where a two-to-four-word query gives
  BM25 the least to match on and it most often misses the gold outright.
- **RRF lost nothing against BM25** (38 gained / 0 lost). Consistent with the
  consensus mechanism above: a document BM25 ranks in its top five stays near the top
  of the fused list unless dense ranks it very low, and on this run that never
  happened. On this corpus RRF is a way to add dense to a lexical system without
  losing anything; it is not a way to add lexical signal to dense.
- **Collapse is unchanged** (1.116 vs 1.106 mean; p90 1.40 both). Fusion did not change
  how many chunks fold into one article.
- **H-010 pending the weighted runs; on this run it holds** (no hybrid beat the control),
  though its reasoning — "nine is the ceiling on what fusion can recover" — was wrong:
  RRF gained 14, five of them questions neither half answered. **H-012 wrong in one
  half:** BM25-only context documents were 1 of 1,000 (under 10%, as written), but the
  candidate-set overlap was 0.196, **not above 0.3**. Resolution written in
  HYPOTHESES.md.

## Anomalies

- **Latency: p50 7,330 ms, p95 18,740 ms** — the dense control's were 298 / 635 ms on the
  same query-embedding call. Checked during the run: three ad-hoc query embeddings
  against DeepInfra took 6.6 s, 7.1 s and 2.5 s. This is the provider's response time
  today (0 retries, all 200 calls answered by DeepInfra), not the fusion — BM25 is
  15 ms and the fusion itself is arithmetic over 200 items. Latency for this axis is
  therefore not comparable to the Axis 2 rows and is reported, not interpreted.

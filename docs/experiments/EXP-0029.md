## Question
Does trimming each retrieved chunk to the sentences bearing on the question — at the
cost of one LLM call per retrieved chunk — preserve answer quality while shortening the
generator's prompt?

## Observations

**The compressor discriminates well and still loses citations.** Measured over the 500
cached completions against the gold labels: it dropped **43.9% of non-gold chunks and
only 8.7% of gold chunks** — a five-fold discrimination ratio, and evidence that the
model is genuinely reading the question rather than trimming indiscriminately. Eight
questions nevertheless lost a gold document outright.

**Dropping gold explains only a third of the damage.** Citation recall fell on 17
questions and rose on 6. Of the 17 that fell, **6 had a gold chunk dropped** by the
compressor. **The other 11 kept every gold chunk and lost the citation anyway.** That is
the more interesting half: the relevant sentences were still in the prompt, and
shortening the document around them made the generator less likely to cite it. Whatever
the model uses to decide a document is worth citing, it is not only the presence of the
answer text — `cited_nothing` rose from 0.0567 to 0.0900 on the same retrieved
documents.

**Refusal did not move at all.** Δ exactly 0.0000, p = 1.000, with five questions
flipping each way. Removing 47% of the context did not make this generator more likely
to say it could not answer, which is mildly reassuring about the refusal behaviour and
mildly worrying about its calibration.

**Step coverage was the prediction and it was wrong.** H-021 expected procedures to lose
steps, on the reasoning that "Click Save." is individually irrelevant but load-bearing.
Step coverage instead moved *up* (+0.0191, p = 0.734) and step order was preserved
slightly more often. The prompt's instruction to keep whole procedures including their
lead-in lines appears to have been obeyed. The damage landed somewhere I had not named.

**The reported 0.467 understates the technique, because of a bug in my own checker
(MIS-035).** The extractive check compared line against line, so a model that reflowed a
multi-line passage into one line was called a rewriter and its chunk kept uncompressed.
Of the 81 chunks it rejected, 35 were fully extractive and only **5** were genuinely
paraphrased. Simulated over the same cached completions — arithmetic on recorded data,
not a re-run — the fixed checker gives **reduction 0.542** and 208 chunks compressed
instead of 150. **The bug worked against this run's finding:** it kept the context longer
and more like the control's, and citation recall fell significantly regardless. A
conservative bug cannot manufacture a loss, so the negative result stands.

## Anomalies
`cited_nothing` rose by 0.0333 against an MDD of 0.023. It is reported here rather than
in the results table because it is a symptom of the citation-recall change rather than an
independent one — the same nine questions drive both.

## Hypothesis
H-021, written before the run, in two separable halves.
- **Part 1 confirmed:** word reduction above 0.40 — measured **0.467** (and 0.542 under
  the corrected checker).
- **Part 2 wrong, and wrong about the mechanism.** I predicted step coverage would fall
  by more than its noise floor and further than citation precision. Step coverage
  **rose** (+0.019, p = 0.734) and neither it nor citation precision reached
  significance. The metric that did move significantly — citation **recall**, −0.088 at
  p = 0.036 — I did not name at all. My reasoning was that an extractive filter cannot
  see that step 4 matters because steps 1–3 preceded it; the prompt's explicit
  instruction to keep whole procedures evidently handled that. What it could not handle
  was dropping **whole documents** (37.4% of chunks), and the generator then citing
  fewer of the ones that remained.

## Decision
**Control retained; not promoted.** The technique buys a 47% shorter prompt and pays
0.088 of citation recall for it, at one extra LLM call per retrieved chunk
($0.0224 per 100 questions on top of the run). For an assistant whose value rests on
pointing the user at the right help article, that is the wrong side of the trade.
